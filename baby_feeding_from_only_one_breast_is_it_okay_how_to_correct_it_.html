Baby feeding from only one breast ? Is it okay ? How to correct it ?

What to know

It is surprisingly common for a baby to favor one breast. Milk may flow faster on one side, a feeding position may feel more comfortable, or your baby may simply have developed a habit. Both breasts do not have to produce identical amounts. A preference is not automatically a feeding problem.

What you can try

Start by looking at the overall picture. Is your baby swallowing during feeds, producing enough wet diapers, and gaining weight? If so, feeding more from one side may still be working. Try offering the less-preferred breast when your baby is calm. A different hold, such as a football hold, can keep your baby's body facing a familiar direction.

When to seek advice

Do not force a frustrated baby to latch. If the preference began suddenly, consider whether your baby seems uncomfortable turning their head or has symptoms of illness. Seek medical advice for persistent breast pain, redness, fever, or a new lump. A lactation consultant can help with milk supply and positioning if you want to encourage feeding on both sides.

A reassuring next step

Feeding works best when the caregiver can notice small changes rather than rushing to finish. A calm environment, comfortable positioning, and time for pauses may help. If a suggestion does not seem to suit your baby, stop and ask a qualified clinician instead of pushing through discomfort.

One more practical tip

Notice whether your baby turns away from one breast only in a particular position or at every feed. That detail can help a lactation consultant work out whether the issue is comfort, milk flow, or something else. If one side produces more milk, the difference may become more noticeable over time. Do not attempt to force equal feeding times to make the breasts match. Instead, focus on a comfortable latch and effective milk transfer. If the less-used breast becomes painfully full, ask about safe ways to relieve pressure. Sudden breast redness with fever deserves prompt medical attention.
