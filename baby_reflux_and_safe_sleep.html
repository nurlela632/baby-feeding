Baby Reflux and Safe Sleep

Spit-up is common

Finding milk on your shoulder after a feed is familiar to many parents. Babies often spit up because their digestive systems are still developing. If your baby feeds comfortably, produces wet diapers, and gains weight, occasional spit-up may be more of a laundry problem than a medical one.

Still, reflux should not be dismissed when a baby seems distressed, refuses feeds, or struggles to grow. A pediatric clinician can help determine whether symptoms need investigation.

Back to sleep, even with reflux

The safest sleep position for babies is on their backs, including babies who spit up. Use a firm, flat, non-inclined mattress in an appropriate crib, bassinet, or play yard. Keep the sleep space free from pillows, loose blankets, soft toys, and positioning devices.

It may seem logical to raise the mattress or put a baby on their side to reduce reflux. Do not do this. Wedges, inclined sleepers, and elevated crib mattresses can create unsafe positions and are not recommended treatments for reflux.

Small changes during feeding

Try allowing breaks during feeds and burping gently if your baby seems uncomfortable. When bottle-feeding, pause when your baby signals they need a rest instead of encouraging them to finish every drop. Holding a baby upright while they are awake and supervised after feeding may help with comfort.

Do not leave a baby sleeping in a swing, car seat, or upright feeding device. If they fall asleep there, move them to a safe, flat sleep surface as soon as practical. Avoid adding cereal to bottles or trying reflux medication without professional advice.

When to call for help

Contact your baby's healthcare professional if vomiting is persistent, feeding becomes painful, or weight gain slows. Blood in vomit or stool also needs medical assessment. Green vomit, breathing trouble, severe sleepiness, or signs of dehydration need urgent medical attention.

If you are unsure what is normal, keep a simple record of feeds and symptoms to discuss at an appointment. You do not have to guess your way through a difficult feeding period.
