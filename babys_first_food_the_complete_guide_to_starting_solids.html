Baby’s First Food - The Complete Guide to Starting Solids

When is your baby ready?

Starting solids can feel like a big milestone, but it does not need to happen on a particular birthday. For most babies, complementary foods begin around six months. Look for steady head control, sitting with support, and an ability to swallow food rather than pushing it straight back out. Breast milk or infant formula remains an important part of nutrition throughout the first year.

A baby watching your dinner or waking more often at night is not, on its own, a reason to start early. If your baby was born prematurely or has feeding difficulties, ask your healthcare professional about timing.

What should you offer first?

There is no magic first food. Soft, iron-rich options are a good place to begin: mashed beans, well-cooked lentils, smooth meat puree, or iron-fortified infant cereal. Add soft vegetables and fruit for variety. Start with a small amount when your baby is awake and relaxed, and let appetite guide how much they eat.

Some babies are curious immediately; others need several tries. Both are normal. Keep offering different flavors without turning meals into a battle.

Make each bite safer

Sit your baby upright and stay close throughout the meal. Serve food in a texture they can manage, moving gradually from smooth or mashed food to soft lumps and finger foods as their skills develop. Whole grapes, nuts, popcorn, hard raw vegetables, and thick spoonfuls of nut butter can be choking hazards.

Do not give honey before twelve months. Avoid unpasteurized foods and do not use cow's milk as the main drink before the first birthday. If your baby has severe eczema or a known food allergy, ask a clinician how to introduce common allergens safely.

Let your baby lead

Opening the mouth or reaching for food can show interest. Turning away, closing the mouth, or becoming distracted may mean they have had enough. You do not need to persuade a baby to finish a bowl. A little mess is part of learning.

If your baby regularly coughs during meals, struggles to swallow, or is not gaining weight as expected, seek a feeding assessment. Otherwise, focus on safe practice, variety, and relaxed family meals.
