Bottle-Feeding Your Baby? Avoid These 5 Common Mistakes

What to know

First, never prop a bottle. Even when you are busy, your baby needs someone holding and watching them during feeds. Second, avoid pushing them to finish every drop. Babies communicate fullness by slowing down, turning away, or releasing the nipple.

What you can try

Third, check whether milk flows too quickly. Gulping, leaking milk, or pulling away can be clues that your baby needs a slower pace. Try a responsive, paced approach, and seek help if coughing or breathing difficulty happens during feeds. Fourth, never guess formula measurements. Follow the manufacturer's preparation and storage directions exactly; adding extra water can be dangerous.

When to seek advice

Fifth, do not let a rigid clock override hunger cues. A baby may need to feed earlier or later than expected, particularly during growth spurts. Hold them in a supported position, allow breaks, and keep mealtimes calm. If feeding regularly seems difficult or weight gain is uncertain, ask your pediatric clinician for advice rather than relying on gadgets or online shortcuts.

A reassuring next step

Keep an eye on the whole picture over several days: feeding behavior, wet diapers, alertness, and growth. One difficult feed rarely tells the entire story. A pediatric clinician can help you decide whether a change is needed and which steps are appropriate for your family.

One more practical tip

It can help to prepare your feeding space before your baby becomes upset: have a clean bottle ready, sit somewhere comfortable, and keep a cloth nearby. A relaxed routine makes it easier to notice changes in breathing, swallowing, or body language. When your baby pauses, you can lower the bottle and wait rather than immediately encouraging more milk. Bottle feeding is also a chance for eye contact and connection, not just a task to complete. If you share feeding duties with relatives or other caregivers, explain the same safe practices to everyone so your baby receives consistent, attentive care.
