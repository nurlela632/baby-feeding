Older babies move differently

A position that worked beautifully during the newborn weeks may feel awkward when your baby grows longer and stronger. Older babies may turn toward sounds, reach for your clothing, or change position mid-feed. This is common, although it can be distracting. Try sitting where you can support your back and let your baby's body rest comfortably. You may need less support under the head but more space for legs and feet.

Adapt familiar holds

Cradle feeding may feel easier as your baby gains head control. Some families prefer a football hold when the baby is restless, while others use a supported upright hold with the baby facing the parent. Keep your baby's airway clear and avoid positions that bend the neck sharply. A baby who repeatedly pulls away may simply be distracted, but pain, poor growth, or difficulty swallowing should be checked by a clinician.

What about unusual positions?

Online videos sometimes show leaning over a baby to breastfeed, sometimes called dangle feeding. It is not necessary for routine nursing and can be tiring or awkward. There is limited evidence that unusual positions solve blocked ducts or other breast problems. If you have a painful lump, fever, or breast redness, seek healthcare advice rather than relying on a video technique as treatment.

Breastfeeding alongside solids

Around six months, many babies begin complementary foods while continuing breast milk or formula. The timing and length of nursing sessions may shift as the baby's routine changes. There is no need to force a fixed position or schedule. Follow hunger cues, check growth over time, and choose a hold that remains comfortable for both of you.

A practical reminder

Different babies respond to different approaches, so advice from another parent may not fit your situation. Focus on safe positioning, your baby's cues, and whether feeding is effective over time. If something feels painful, confusing, or consistently difficult, ask for individualized help. A brief feeding assessment can often clarify what is happening and what to try next.
