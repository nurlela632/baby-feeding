What to know

A comfortable latch makes breastfeeding easier for both parent and baby. Bring your baby close with the head and body aligned, and wait for a wide-open mouth before guiding them toward the breast. Persistent pain is not something you simply have to tolerate. A lactation professional can help adjust positioning and check for feeding difficulties.

What you can try

When milk is transferring, sucking often becomes deeper and more rhythmic, with pauses for swallowing. You may hear quiet swallows or see movement near your baby's jaw. Early rapid sucking may help milk start flowing. Patterns change during a feed, so do not expect every minute to look identical.

When to seek advice

Babies also suck for comfort, closeness, and reassurance. Comfort sucking may involve lighter movements and fewer swallows; it is not automatically a problem. What matters is whether your baby also gets enough milk across the day. Check wet diapers and growth rather than trying to count every swallow. Seek help if feeds are consistently painful, your baby rarely seems to swallow, or weight gain is concerning.

A reassuring next step

Every family develops a different routine, and it is reasonable to adjust that routine as your baby grows. If something feels consistently difficult, a healthcare professional can observe an actual feed and offer advice based on your baby's age, growth, and medical history. That is more useful than comparing your baby with someone else's schedule.

One more practical tip

If a latch feels pinchy or painful, gently release suction with a clean finger and try again rather than waiting for the discomfort to pass. Bringing the baby toward you, instead of leaning forward, may help with positioning. Support your arms and back with pillows if needed. It is normal for feeding patterns to change as babies grow and become more efficient. Comfort sucking may continue after active swallowing slows, and families can decide what feels manageable. If you notice persistent nipple damage, a baby who falls asleep immediately at every feed, or poor weight gain, request a feeding assessment.
