Do This After Breast Feeding Your New Born Baby! Dr Imran Patel

A quiet moment after a feed

When your newborn finishes breastfeeding, you do not need an elaborate routine. Notice whether your baby seems relaxed, still shows hunger cues, or needs a brief pause. Some babies want to burp, while others seem comfortable without it. If you choose to burp, support the head and neck and pat or rub gently. Avoid vigorous bouncing or pressure on the stomach. A little spit-up can be normal, especially when your baby is otherwise comfortable and gaining weight.

Check your own comfort too

If your breasts feel sore or your nipples are damaged after feeding, do not ignore persistent pain. Latch and positioning problems are common reasons to ask for lactation support. Keep a drink nearby and take a moment to rest if you can. If your baby repeatedly wants to feed again soon, remember that cluster feeding is common in the newborn period. Frequent feeding does not automatically mean your milk supply is low. Wet diapers and weight checks provide stronger clues.

Make the next sleep safe

After feeding, place your baby on their back on a firm, flat, clear sleep surface in an appropriate crib or bassinet. This advice also applies to babies who spit up. Do not use wedges, raised mattresses, or inclined sleepers to prevent reflux. If you are drowsy, ask another caregiver to help return the baby to their own sleep space. Seek medical advice if vomiting is persistent, feeding is difficult, or your baby is unusually hard to wake. The simplest post-feed routine is often the safest.

One more thing to remember

If your baby is uncomfortable after most feeds, consider recording what happens and discussing it with a clinician. Do not start medicines or change feeding products solely because an online video recommends them. Individual circumstances matter.
