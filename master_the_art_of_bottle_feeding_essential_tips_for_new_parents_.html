Master the Art of Bottle-Feeding: Essential Tips for New Parents! 🍼

Start by getting comfortable

Choose a seat where you can support your baby's head and neck without straining your arms. Hold your baby close and fairly upright, not flat on their back. Let the baby open their mouth before offering the nipple. Never prop a bottle or leave your baby feeding unattended. You can make eye contact and talk softly while feeding; it is a chance to connect, not just a task to finish. Keep a cloth nearby and allow enough time for pauses.

Follow your baby's pace

Some babies gulp when the nipple flow is too fast, while others need a moment to coordinate sucking and swallowing. Try holding the bottle at a gentle angle and offering breaks when your baby slows down or pulls away. A slower-flow nipple may help some babies, but brands label flow differently. Stop when your baby shows fullness rather than encouraging the last few drops. If coughing, choking, or breathing difficulty happens repeatedly during feeds, seek professional assessment.

Handle milk carefully

Wash hands and clean equipment as directed. If you use powdered formula, measure water and powder exactly according to the label. Do not add extra water or use breast milk instead of the required mixing water. Follow storage and discard instructions for formula and expressed milk, which are not identical. If your baby was born prematurely or has a medical condition, ask your healthcare team about extra precautions. Bottle feeding becomes easier with practice, and there is no need to aim for perfection at every feed.

One more thing to remember

Choose a bottle and nipple that your baby manages comfortably, not one advertised as universally best. If feeding is persistently stressful, a pediatric feeding specialist can assess the situation. Buying more equipment is not always the answer.
