Your favorite may change

New parents are often shown a single breastfeeding position and told to keep practicing. In reality, comfort can change from one feed to the next. Your baby's size, your recovery after birth, and even the chair you use can affect how a hold feels. Begin by relaxing your shoulders and bringing your baby close, with their head and body supported. Avoid leaning forward to reach the baby; that can strain your neck and back.

When extra support helps

The cross-cradle hold can make it easier to guide a newborn toward a deep latch because the opposite arm supports their shoulders. A football hold keeps the baby's body beside you and may feel more comfortable after a cesarean birth. Some parents prefer a reclined position that lets the baby rest against them. If one position causes pain, do not assume you simply need to endure it. Adjust the hold and seek feeding support if discomfort persists.

Follow the baby's signals

Watch your baby open their mouth widely before bringing them to the breast. Their chin can touch the breast while the nose remains clear. During active feeding, you may notice deep jaw movements and swallowing. A baby who repeatedly slips off or struggles to swallow may need more than a new position. A pediatric clinician or lactation consultant can check whether milk transfer and weight gain are going well.

Make the space work for you

Keep water and a cloth within reach, support your arms with cushions if needed, and choose a place where you can stay awake. If you become drowsy while nursing, plan to return your baby to their own firm, flat, clear sleep surface as soon as feeding ends. Your preferred position should help both of you feel secure, not make feeding a test of endurance.

A practical reminder

Feeding routines change quickly in the first year. What worked last month may need adjusting as your baby grows and develops new skills. Stay flexible, keep safety at the center of decisions, and avoid judging progress by a single meal. If your baby has a medical condition or growth concerns, follow the plan from their own healthcare team.
